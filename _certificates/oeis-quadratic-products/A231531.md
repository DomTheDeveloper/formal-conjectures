# The prime 2 and the oscillating primes of OEIS A231531

Let $Y_n=\operatorname{Im}\prod_{k=1}^n(k+i)$, the sequence [A231531](https://oeis.org/A231531). Three assertions in the entry follow from an exact valuation law and a pairing argument.

1. The predicted slope at 2 holds over all positive indices: $v_2(Y_n)=n/4+O(\log(2n))$.
2. Every positive integer $m$ divides the nonzero term $Y_{2m}$. In particular the set of type-one primes is empty even when divisibility of zero is excluded.
3. The oscillating primes are exactly $p\equiv3\pmod4$, when oscillation means that every tail contains a term coprime to $p$ and nonzero terms divisible by arbitrarily high powers of $p$.

### The coordinate change

Write $P_n=A_n+iB_n=\prod_{k=1}^n(1+ki)$. Since $k+i=i(1-ki)$, the product defining $Y_n$ equals $i^n\overline{P_n}$. For even $n$ this gives $Y_n=\pm B_n$; for odd $n$ it gives $Y_n=\pm A_n$.

Use the [existing A105751 exact four-class theorem by KitaKen1](https://github.com/KitaKen1/oeis-a105751-two-adic/tree/a9692d90078c4dff1478acaaa70be2fba06a3ef9). In particular, $v_2(B_{4m})=m+2+v_2(\lceil m/2\rceil)$ for $m\ge1$, $v_2(B_{4m+2})=m$, and $v_2(B_{4m+3})\ge m+3$ for $m\ge1$. The last term at $m=0$ is $B_3=0$.

The norm $A_n^2+B_n^2=\prod_{k=1}^n(1+k^2)$ has 2-adic valuation $\lceil n/2\rceil$: every odd $k$ contributes exactly one power and every even $k$ contributes none. If two squares have equal finite valuation $2l$, their sum has valuation $2l+1$ because odd squares are 1 modulo 8. Unequal valuations give the smaller valuation. At $n=4m+1$ the norm has odd valuation $2m+1$, so $v_2(A_{4m+1})=m$. At $n=4m+3$ the norm has valuation $2m+2$ and $B_n^2$ is divisible by $2^{2m+3}$, including at $n=3$. Hence $v_2(A_{4m+3})=m+1$.

Combining the coordinates proves
$$
\begin{aligned}
v_2(Y_{4m}) &= m+2+v_2(\lceil m/2\rceil) &&(m\ge1),\\
v_2(Y_{4m+1}) &=m,\qquad v_2(Y_{4m+2})=m,\qquad
v_2(Y_{4m+3})=m+1 &&(m\ge0).
\end{aligned}
$$
Every displayed term is nonzero. The possible correction to $n/4$ grows at most logarithmically. This proves clause (i) at 2. The split odd prime growth question remains separate.

### Divisors and oscillation

In $P_{2m}$ pair indices $k$ and $2m-k$ for $1\le k<m$. Their factors are conjugate modulo $m$, and the factors at $m$ and $2m$ are both 1 modulo $m$. Thus $m\mid B_{2m}$, and the coordinate change gives $m\mid Y_{2m}$. The valuation formula ensures the witness is nonzero. Taking arbitrarily large multiples of $p^e$ for $m$ supplies nonzero terms divisible by $p^e$ on every tail.

If $p\equiv3\pmod4$, no factor $1+k^2$ vanishes modulo $p$. At an index $2m$ with $p\mid m$, the product defining $Y_{2m}$ therefore has nonzero norm and zero imaginary coefficient modulo $p$. Its real coefficient is a unit. Multiplication by $(2m+1)+i$ makes the next imaginary coefficient that same unit modulo $p$.

If $p\equiv1\pmod4$, some $1+k^2$ vanishes for $1\le k<p$. The norm at $2p$ is then zero modulo $p$, and $Y_{2p}=0\pmod p$ by the pairing. These two facts force the real coordinate to be zero as well. All later products have both coordinates divisible by $p$. At $p=2$, the exact formula makes every $Y_n$ even from $n=3$ onward. This completes the classification under the stated interpretation.

### Independent check

The code works directly with multiplication by $n+i$. It checks the exact positive-index formula and divisor witnesses, then checks unit witnesses or tail extinction for every odd prime through 300.

```python
from math import isqrt

def v2(x):
    assert x != 0
    return (x & -x).bit_length()-1

a,b=1,0
for n in range(1,4097):
    a,b=n*a-b,a+n*b
    m,r=divmod(n,4)
    predicted=(m+2+v2((m+1)//2) if r==0 else m+(r==3))
    assert v2(b)==predicted
    if n%2==0:
        assert b%(n//2)==0

for p in range(3,301,2):
    if any(p%k==0 for k in range(2,isqrt(p)+1)):
        continue
    a,b=1,0
    for n in range(1,6*p+2):
        a,b=(n*a-b)%p,(a+n*b)%p
        if p%4==3 and n%(2*p)==0:
            assert a and b==0
        if p%4==3 and n%(2*p)==1:
            assert b
        if p%4==1 and n>=2*p:
            assert a==b==0
print("Passed 4096 exact terms and all odd primes through 300.")
```
